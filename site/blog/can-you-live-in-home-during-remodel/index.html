Can You Live in Your Home During a Remodel? A Tampa Contractor's Honest Answer

Home / Blog / Can You Live In Home During Remodel

The short answer: it depends on the scope. A kitchen remodel? Yes — you'll lose your kitchen for 2-6 weeks but you can stay in the house. A whole-home renovation? Usually no — the disruption is too extensive for most families. A bathroom remodel? Yes, if you have a second bathroom.

We've managed hundreds of remodeling projects in Tampa where homeowners stayed in the house and hundreds where they moved out temporarily. Here's what you should actually expect, by project type.

Author: Mason Homes Editorial Team

By Project Type

Kitchen Remodel — Yes, You Can Stay

Duration of disruption: 2-6 weeks without a functioning kitchen (4-8 weeks total project time for mid-range; 8-12 weeks for full gut renovations per Estimate Florida Consulting)

You'll live without a working sink, stove, and dishwasher for the active construction phase. Most homeowners set up a temporary kitchen in another room:

  • Essentials: Microwave, mini-fridge, electric kettle, toaster oven, paper plates. Set up on a folding table in the dining room or guest bedroom.
  • Water access: Use the bathroom sink for filling pots and washing dishes. It's inconvenient but manageable.
  • Budget for eating out: Most families spend an extra $500-$1,500 on takeout and dining during a kitchen remodel. Factor this into your project budget.

Noise and dust: Demolition (1-3 days) is the loudest phase. Cabinet installation, countertop templating, and finish work are moderate noise. We use plastic barriers and zip walls between the kitchen and living areas to contain dust. See our full process →

Tip: If your kitchen remodel involves only cabinet refacing and countertop replacement (no plumbing or electrical), you may only lose kitchen access for 10-15 business days.

Bathroom Remodel — Yes, If You Have Another Bathroom

Duration of disruption: 3-5 weeks for a standard bathroom, 5-8 weeks for a master bath (per multiple Tampa contractor sources)

If you have 2 or more bathrooms, staying home during a bathroom remodel is straightforward. You lose access to 1 bathroom; you use the other. The only real inconvenience is sharing a bathroom with more family members than usual.

If you only have 1 bathroom, you have a problem. Options:

  • Portable toilet rental: $150-$300/month. Not ideal for most families, but some homeowners use this for the 3-5 day period when the toilet is disconnected.
  • Gym membership: $30-$50/month gives you access to showers. Several Tampa homeowners have told us this was their go-to solution during single-bathroom remodels.
  • Stay with family or friends: For the 1-2 weeks when the bathroom is completely non-functional (demo through rough-in), staying elsewhere is the most practical option.

Whole-Home Renovation — Usually No

Duration of disruption: 3-6 months for a full whole-home renovation (per multiple sources)

When every room is being touched — new flooring throughout, all bathrooms gutted, kitchen remodeled, walls opened, electrical and plumbing updated — living in the home is impractical for most families. Here's why:

  • No functional kitchen or bathroom for extended periods
  • Dust everywhere — even with barriers, drywall dust, sawdust, and construction debris migrate through the house over 3-6 months
  • Safety hazards — exposed wiring, open subfloors, removed handrails, power tools, and construction materials throughout the home
  • Noise — 7:00 AM to 5:00 PM, 5 days a week, for months. Demolition, sawing, hammering, compressors, and radio.
  • Strangers in your home — a crew of 3-8 workers in and out of every room, every day, for months

Our recommendation: Plan for temporary housing during a whole-home renovation. Budget $2,000-$5,000/month for a short-term rental in the Tampa area, or $4,000-$8,000/month for a furnished apartment. Total temporary housing for a 4-month project: $8,000-$32,000. Include this in your renovation budget from the start.

Home Addition — Usually Yes

Duration of disruption: 8-16 weeks for a standard room addition (per HomeGuide and Tampa contractor estimates)

Additions are new construction attached to your existing home. During most of the build, the addition is separated from the living space by the existing exterior wall. The disruption is limited:

  • Foundation and framing — Exterior work. Noisy but doesn't affect the interior.
  • Rough-in — Plumbing, electrical, and HVAC are run in the new space. Minor disruption when connecting to existing systems (1-2 days of water or power interruption).
  • The breakthrough — When the existing wall is opened to connect the addition, there's a 1-3 day period of significant dust and noise in the adjacent room. We seal off the opening with plastic until the connection is complete.
  • Finish work — Painting, flooring, and trim in the new space. Minimal disruption to existing rooms.

Bottom line: you can live in the home during an addition. The noisiest phases happen outside or in the sealed-off new space.

Dust Containment — What to Expect

Dust is the number one complaint from homeowners who live through a remodel. Here's what we do and what you should expect:

  • Zip walls and plastic barriers — Floor-to-ceiling plastic sheets with zipper doors seal the work area from living spaces. These are standard on every Mason Homes project.
  • Negative air pressure — We use fans and air scrubbers to pull air from the work area and exhaust it outside, preventing dust from migrating to your living spaces.
  • Floor protection — Ram board or heavy paper covers floors in hallways and rooms adjacent to the work zone.
  • Daily cleanup — Our crew cleans the work area at the end of every day. Debris goes in a dumpster, surfaces are wiped, and tools are organized.

Reality check: Even with all these measures, fine dust will reach other parts of the house. You'll notice a light film on surfaces 2-3 rooms away. Budget 4-6 hours for a deep clean (or $200-$400 for a professional cleaning service) after the project is complete.

Noise and Work Hours

Tampa's noise ordinances allow residential construction from 7:00 AM to 6:00 PM Monday through Friday and 8:00 AM to 6:00 PM on Saturdays. Most remodeling work happens between 7:30 AM and 4:30 PM on weekdays.

Loudest phases: - Demolition — jackhammers (for tile removal), reciprocating saws, sledgehammers. 1-3 days per area. - Framing — nail guns, circular saws. Intermittent throughout the day. - Tile cutting — wet saws are loud and constant during tile installation days.

Quieter phases: - Painting — minimal noise - Cabinet installation — moderate (drills, occasional saw cuts) - Countertop installation — 2-4 hours of moderate noise - Finish trim — moderate

If you work from home, plan to work from a coffee shop or coworking space during demolition and framing days. Take calls from another location. Noise-canceling headphones help during lighter phases but won't cut it during demo.

Pets and Children

Pets: Keep dogs and cats in a closed room away from the work zone or at a friend's house on demolition days. Open doors, power tools, and exposed nails are real hazards. A nervous dog can bolt through an open exterior door during a delivery. Dust and debris irritate pet respiratory systems. Budget $30-$50/day for doggy daycare on the loudest days.

Children: Kids under 5 should not be in the house during active demolition. Exposed nails, dust, and power tools are serious safety risks. School-age children can be home during quieter phases if they stay out of the work zone. Set clear boundaries and discuss them with the crew lead.

Tampa-Specific Considerations

Temporary Housing in Tampa

If you need to move out during a whole-home renovation, Tampa offers several options:

  • Short-term rentals (Airbnb/VRBO): $80-$200/night in the Tampa area. Look in your same neighborhood to keep school commutes and routines intact.
  • Furnished apartments: $2,000-$4,000/month for a 1-2 bedroom. Corporate housing companies serve the Tampa market. 3-month minimum lease is common.
  • Extended-stay hotels: $1,500-$3,000/month. Residence Inn, Homewood Suites, and similar brands offer weekly and monthly rates.
  • Family or friends: Free, but a 4-month stay tests any relationship. Have an honest conversation about timeline and expectations.

Storing Belongings in Florida Humidity

If you're packing up a room for renovation, where you store items matters in Tampa:

  • Climate-controlled storage units: $100-$250/month for a 10x10 unit. Required for furniture, electronics, clothing, and anything moisture-sensitive. Non-climate-controlled storage in Tampa's heat and humidity will grow mold on leather, warp wood furniture, and damage electronics.
  • Garage storage: Acceptable for a few weeks if items are elevated off the floor and covered with tarps. Not recommended for longer than 1 month in summer.
  • PODS or portable containers: $150-$300/month. Convenient (delivered to your driveway) but not climate-controlled. Use desiccant packets and raise items off the container floor.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I live in my house during a kitchen remodel? Yes. You'll lose kitchen access for 2-6 weeks. Set up a temporary kitchen with a microwave, mini-fridge, and electric kettle in another room. Budget an extra $500-$1,500 for takeout and dining during the remodel. Demolition (1-3 days) is the loudest phase.

How long does it take to remodel a whole home in Tampa? A full whole-home renovation takes 3-6 months depending on scope. Mid-range whole-home projects ($100,000-$250,000) typically run 4-5 months. We recommend temporary housing for the duration — budget $2,000-$5,000/month for short-term rental in Tampa.

How much dust does a remodel produce? Significant dust during demolition and drywall phases. We use zip walls, negative air pressure, and daily cleanup to contain it. Even so, expect a light dust film on surfaces 2-3 rooms away. Budget $200-$400 for professional post-renovation cleaning.

What time do construction crews start work in Tampa? Tampa allows residential construction starting at 7:00 AM Monday through Friday and 8:00 AM on Saturdays. Most crews work 7:30 AM to 4:30 PM on weekdays.

Should I board my pets during a remodel? During demolition days (1-3 days per area), yes — remove pets from the home or confine them to a closed room far from the work zone. During quieter phases (painting, trim work), pets can stay home if they're kept away from the construction area. Budget $30-$50/day for doggy daycare on loud days.
