Open Concept Kitchen Remodel in Tampa — Cost, Layout & What to Expect

Home / Blog / Open Concept Kitchen Remodel Tampa

Removing the wall between a kitchen and living or dining area is the single most impactful layout change in a Tampa home renovation. It changes how the space feels, how the family uses it, and how the home appraises. It also adds structural complexity, permit requirements, and cost that a standard kitchen remodel does not.

An open concept kitchen remodel in Tampa typically costs $35,000-$95,000 total — that includes the kitchen renovation itself plus the structural wall removal. The wall removal portion adds $8,000-$25,000 depending on whether the wall is load-bearing and what mechanical systems run through it (per Estimate Florida Consulting, NovaCoreBuilders, 2026).

This guide covers the structural considerations, cost breakdown, permit process, and timeline for opening up a kitchen in a Tampa-area home.

What "Open Concept" Actually Means in Remodeling

Open concept kitchen remodeling means removing part or all of the wall that separates the kitchen from an adjacent living room, dining room, or family room. The result is a single combined space where the kitchen flows into the main living area.

There are three common approaches:

  1. Full wall removal — The entire wall comes out, replaced by a structural beam if load-bearing. This creates the most dramatic change and the largest open sightline.
  2. Partial wall removal — A section of wall is removed (typically creating a 6-10 foot opening) while leaving portions of the wall on one or both sides. This maintains some visual separation and reduces structural costs.
  3. Pass-through or bar opening — A counter-height opening (typically 42 inches high) is cut into the wall. This is the least expensive option and does not require a full structural beam if the remaining wall can support the load above.

Load-Bearing vs. Non-Load-Bearing Walls

This is the most important factor in your project cost and complexity. Every wall in your home is either load-bearing (it supports the weight of the roof, ceiling, or floor above) or non-load-bearing (it serves only as a room divider).

How to Identify Load-Bearing Walls

A licensed structural engineer makes the definitive determination, but general indicators include:

  • Walls that run perpendicular to the roof trusses or ceiling joists are more likely to be load-bearing
  • Exterior walls are almost always load-bearing
  • Walls directly below a beam in the attic are typically load-bearing
  • Walls that sit on top of a foundation wall or footer below are often structural

In Tampa's CBS (concrete block and stucco) homes — which make up the majority of houses built from the 1960s through the 1990s — interior walls are frequently non-load-bearing wood-framed partitions within the concrete block exterior shell. This is good news for open concept conversions: many interior kitchen walls in Tampa CBS homes can be removed without major structural work.

However, you cannot assume. Some interior walls in CBS homes are load-bearing, particularly in homes with hip roofs or second stories. The only way to be certain is a structural engineer's assessment.

Structural Engineer Assessment

A licensed structural engineer will inspect the wall, review the home's construction, and determine:

  • Whether the wall is load-bearing
  • What size and type of beam (LVL, steel, or glulam) is required as a replacement header
  • What type of support columns or posts are needed at the beam ends
  • Whether the foundation can support the concentrated point loads from the new beam

Cost: $300-$800 for a residential structural engineer's report in Tampa (per Estimate Florida Consulting). This is a required step before any wall removal permit will be issued.

Permits for Wall Removal in Tampa

Wall removal — whether load-bearing or not — requires a building permit from the City of Tampa or Hillsborough County (per City of Tampa Construction Services, tampa.gov/guide/permits). Here is what the permit process looks like:

What You Need to Submit

  • Architectural drawings showing existing and proposed floor plans
  • Structural engineer's report and beam calculations (for load-bearing walls)
  • Electrical plan if circuits are being rerouted
  • Plumbing plan if pipes are being relocated

Permit Cost

$200-$800 depending on the total project value (per City of Tampa Construction Services). Electrical and plumbing permits are filed separately from the general building permit.

Plan Review Timeline

10-15 business days for standard residential permits (Hillsborough County) (per Hillsborough County Building Services). Projects involving structural changes may take closer to the 10-day end.

Required Inspections

  1. Framing inspection — After the old wall is removed and the new beam/header is installed
  2. Rough-in inspection — After electrical and plumbing rerouting is complete
  3. Final inspection — After all work is finished, including drywall, flooring, and finishes

Tampa Floor Plans That Benefit Most

Certain home styles common in the Tampa Bay area are strong candidates for open concept kitchen conversions.

1980s-1990s Suburban CBS Homes

Homes in FishHawk, Bloomingdale, Carrollwood, Riverview, and Brandon built during this era frequently have closed-off kitchens with a wall separating the kitchen from the family room. These homes often have non-load-bearing interior partition walls within a CBS (concrete block) exterior, making wall removal more straightforward and less expensive.

1960s-1970s Tampa Ranch Homes

Single-story ranch-style homes in South Tampa, Temple Terrace, and Town 'N Country often have galley or U-shaped kitchens. Opening these into the adjacent dining or living room creates a significantly larger-feeling space. Many of these homes have hip roofs, so interior walls may be load-bearing — always verify with an engineer.

1990s-2000s Two-Story Homes

New Tampa, Wesley Chapel, and Westchase homes from this era sometimes have first-floor walls that support the second floor. Wall removal in these homes requires more extensive structural engineering but is still very common.

Design Considerations After the Wall Comes Out

Once the wall is removed, several design decisions follow:

  • Kitchen island placement — An island often replaces the wall as the visual divider between kitchen and living space. Budget $3,000-$12,000 for a custom island with countertop, depending on size and features.
  • Flooring continuity — Matching or coordinating the kitchen and living room flooring creates a cohesive look. LVP is the most popular choice in Tampa for continuous flooring through open concept spaces — it handles humidity and is waterproof (per Estimate Florida Consulting).
  • Lighting zones — Separate lighting circuits for the kitchen and living area allow independent control. Recessed lighting, pendant fixtures over an island, and living room fixtures should be on different switches.
  • Ventilation — With no wall to contain cooking odors, a powerful range hood (400+ CFM) vented to the exterior becomes more important.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does it cost to remove a wall between the kitchen and living room in Tampa? Removing a non-load-bearing wall costs $2,800-$8,000 including demolition, electrical rerouting, drywall repair, and flooring transitions. Removing a load-bearing wall costs $8,000-$25,000 including structural engineering ($300-$800), beam installation ($3,000-$12,000), and associated work (per Estimate Florida Consulting, NovaCoreBuilders, 2026).

Do I need a permit to remove a kitchen wall in Tampa? Yes. Wall removal — whether load-bearing or non-load-bearing — requires a building permit from the City of Tampa or Hillsborough County. Permit costs range from $200-$800 depending on project value. Structural changes require a structural engineer's signed and sealed report as part of the permit application (per City of Tampa Construction Services).

How do I know if my kitchen wall is load-bearing? A licensed structural engineer makes the definitive determination. General indicators: walls running perpendicular to roof trusses, walls sitting on a foundation wall below, and walls in multi-story homes that align with walls on the floor above. A residential structural assessment costs $300-$800 in Tampa. Do not rely on visual inspection alone.

How long does an open concept kitchen remodel take in Tampa? A mid-range open concept kitchen remodel with load-bearing wall removal takes 8-14 weeks from demolition to final inspection. A standard kitchen remodel without wall removal takes 4-8 weeks. The wall removal adds 1-2 weeks to the construction timeline, plus 1-2 weeks for structural engineering before permits are submitted.

Will removing a kitchen wall increase my home value? Open floor plans are consistently preferred by Tampa Bay homebuyers. A mid-range kitchen remodel returns 60-80% of its cost at resale (per national remodeling data adapted to the Tampa market). The open concept layout itself does not have a standalone ROI figure, but homes with open kitchens sell faster and appraise more favorably than comparable homes with closed-off kitchens in the same Tampa neighborhoods.

Are most interior walls in Tampa CBS homes load-bearing? No. In CBS (concrete block and stucco) construction — the most common building method in Tampa from the 1960s through the 1990s — most interior walls are non-load-bearing wood-framed partitions. The concrete block exterior walls carry the structural load. However, some interior walls do support roof trusses or second-floor loads, so a structural engineer's assessment is always required before wall removal.
