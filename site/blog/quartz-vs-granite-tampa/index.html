Quartz vs Granite Countertops in Tampa — Cost, Durability & Maintenance

Home / Blog / Quartz Vs Granite Tampa

Granite and quartz are the two most popular countertop materials in Tampa kitchens. Granite starts at $27 per square foot installed (per AGS Stone, Tampa). Quartz starts at $50 per square foot installed (per multiple Tampa fabricators). Both are durable, both look good, and both hold resale value — but they perform differently in Tampa's climate and they require different maintenance.

This guide compares the two side by side, with Tampa-specific pricing, humidity considerations, and a note on marble for homeowners considering a premium alternative.

Granite Countertops — What Tampa Homeowners Should Know

Granite is a natural stone quarried from the earth. Every slab is one of a kind — the veining, color variation, and mineral flecking are unique. This is granite's biggest selling point and its biggest limitation: you're choosing from what nature produced, not a catalog of uniform patterns.

Granite Pricing in Tampa

Granite starts at approximately $27 per square foot installed (per AGS Stone, Tampa). Mid-range granite runs $40-$60/sq ft. Exotic or rare granite slabs can reach $80-$100+/sq ft. For a typical Tampa kitchen with 40-50 square feet of countertop, that's $1,080-$5,000+ for the countertop material and installation.

Granite and Florida Humidity

Tampa's average humidity is 74%. Granite is a porous stone — it has microscopic holes that can absorb liquids and moisture. When properly sealed, this isn't a problem. When the seal wears down (every 12-24 months, depending on use), granite becomes vulnerable to staining and, in rare cases, can develop mold in the pores.

Annual sealing takes about 30 minutes and costs $15-$30 for a DIY sealer product. Professional resealing runs $100-$200 per visit. If you stay on top of sealing, granite performs well in Florida. If you forget for 2-3 years, you may see water spots or staining around the sink area.

Granite Heat Resistance

Granite is formed under extreme heat and pressure. You can set a hot pan directly on a granite countertop without damage. This is granite's clear advantage over quartz — no trivets needed. For homeowners who cook frequently and want to move pots from stove to counter without thinking about it, granite wins on this point.

Granite Appearance

No two granite slabs are identical. The variation includes veining, flecking, color shifts, and crystalline structures visible in the stone. Some homeowners love this; others find it hard to match with cabinet colors and backsplash patterns. Always view the full slab in person before purchasing — small samples don't represent the full variation.

Quartz Countertops — What Tampa Homeowners Should Know

Quartz countertops are engineered stone: approximately 93% ground natural quartz crystals bound with 7% polymer resin and pigments. Major manufacturers include Caesarstone, Cambria, Silestone, and MSI. The engineering process allows for consistent color, pattern, and performance across every slab.

Quartz Pricing in Tampa

Quartz starts at $50 per square foot installed (per multiple Tampa fabricators and industry sources). Mid-range quartz runs $60-$80/sq ft. Premium brands and patterns (marble-look, large-format veining) reach $80-$120/sq ft. For 40-50 square feet of countertop, budget $2,000-$6,000+.

Quartz and Florida Humidity

This is where quartz separates itself in the Tampa market. Quartz is non-porous — zero absorption of liquids, moisture, or humidity. It will not develop mold, mildew, or staining from Florida's 74% average humidity. No sealing required. No special cleaners needed. Soap, water, and a soft cloth handle all routine cleaning.

For bathrooms, outdoor kitchens (under cover), and kitchens near pools or water, quartz's zero-maintenance performance in humid conditions is a significant advantage.

Quartz Heat Resistance

Here's quartz's weakness: the resin binder can scorch or discolor at temperatures above 300°F. Setting a hot pan directly from the stove onto a quartz countertop can leave a permanent mark. Use a trivet or hot pad. This isn't a defect — it's a limitation of the resin component. If you forget once, you may have a white or yellow ring that can't be polished out.

Quartz Appearance

Quartz offers the widest range of patterns available in any countertop material. Manufacturers produce convincing marble looks (Calacatta, Carrara patterns), concrete looks, solid colors, and traditional granite-inspired patterns — all with consistent coloring from slab to slab. If you want your kitchen island and your perimeter counters to match perfectly, quartz delivers. Granite cannot guarantee that level of consistency.

What About Marble?

Marble is the premium natural stone alternative. Tampa pricing runs $60-$150 per square foot installed (per multiple industry sources). Marble is heat-resistant, visually striking, and carries strong resale value — but it's softer than both granite and quartz (3-5 on the Mohs scale), stains easily without frequent sealing, and can etch from acidic foods like lemon juice, tomato sauce, and vinegar.

Marble works best in low-use areas: a butler's pantry, a bathroom vanity, or a baking station where it stays cool for rolling dough. For a primary kitchen work surface in a Tampa home, marble requires more maintenance than most homeowners want to commit to. If you love the marble look, a quartz countertop with a Calacatta or Carrara pattern gives you the visual without the upkeep.

Which Is Better for Tampa Kitchens?

There's no universal answer — it depends on your priorities:

Choose granite if: - You want a natural, one-of-a-kind stone look - You regularly place hot pans directly on the counter - You're comfortable with annual sealing (30 minutes per year) - Budget is a primary factor — granite starts $23/sq ft lower than quartz

Choose quartz if: - You want zero maintenance — no sealing, no special cleaners - Humidity resistance matters (especially for bathroom countertops or kitchens in high-moisture areas) - You want consistent color and pattern matching across all your countertop sections - You're willing to use trivets for hot cookware

For Tampa specifically: Quartz is the more popular choice in recent years, driven by its humidity resistance and low maintenance. In our experience, roughly 65% of Tampa kitchen remodel clients choose quartz, 25% choose granite, and 10% choose marble or other materials.

Countertop Installation Timeline and Process

Both granite and quartz follow a similar installation process:

  1. Template — A fabricator visits your kitchen after cabinets are installed and creates an exact template of your countertop layout using a laser measuring device or physical templates. Takes 30-60 minutes.
  2. Fabrication — The slab is cut, edged, and polished at the fabrication shop. Sink cutouts, cooktop cutouts, and edge profiles are finished. Takes 10-15 business days.
  3. Installation — Fabricated countertops are delivered and installed. Seams are joined with color-matched epoxy. Sink is set and connected (by plumber). Takes 2-4 hours for a standard kitchen.

Total timeline from template to installed countertop: 2-3 weeks for both granite and quartz. This timeline runs concurrently with other kitchen remodel phases — countertops are installed after cabinets and before backsplash.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is quartz or granite cheaper in Tampa? Granite is cheaper. Granite starts at $27 per square foot installed (per AGS Stone, Tampa), while quartz starts at $50 per square foot installed. For a 50-square-foot kitchen countertop, that's a difference of at least $1,150 in material and installation costs.

Does quartz hold up in Florida humidity? Yes. Quartz is non-porous and does not absorb moisture, making it ideal for Florida's 74% average humidity. It requires zero sealing and will not develop mold, mildew, or moisture-related staining. Granite, by contrast, requires annual sealing to resist humidity and moisture absorption.

Can you put hot pans on granite? Yes. Granite is formed under extreme geological heat and pressure and can handle direct contact with hot cookware without damage. Quartz cannot — the resin binder in quartz can scorch or discolor above 300°F. Always use trivets on quartz countertops.

How long do quartz countertops last? Quartz countertops last 25-50 years or more with normal use. They are resistant to scratching, staining, and chipping. The most common damage is heat discoloration from hot pans (preventable with trivets) and edge chipping from impact.

Which countertop material has better resale value? Both granite and quartz are considered premium countertop materials by home buyers. Quartz has gained ground in recent years and is now the preferred material for approximately 65% of kitchen remodels nationwide. Either material will positively impact resale compared to laminate or tile countertops, which are valued $5,000-$15,000 less in comparable Tampa homes.

How often does granite need to be sealed? Granite should be sealed every 12-24 months. Sealing takes about 30 minutes for a standard kitchen and costs $15-$30 for a DIY sealer or $100-$200 for professional resealing. In Tampa's humid climate, sealing on the 12-month end of that range is advisable.
