Neighborhoods We Serve in South Tampa

Hyde Park (33606)

Hyde Park is Tampa's premier historic district, with homes dating to the 1920s-1940s. Bungalows, Craftsman cottages, and Mediterranean Revival homes line streets shaded by mature oaks. The Hyde Park Architectural Review Commission (ARC) reviews all exterior modifications — including window replacements, additions, roofline changes, and exterior paint colors. We handle ARC applications as part of the permitting process. Interior remodels (kitchens, bathrooms, flooring) do not require ARC review.

Davis Islands (33606)

A waterfront community on two man-made islands south of downtown Tampa. Homes are a mix of 1920s Mediterranean Revival originals and modern rebuilds. Many properties sit in FEMA flood zones AE — additions and significant renovations require elevated construction and flood-resistant materials below the base flood elevation (BFE). Davis Islands kitchens and bathrooms tend toward high-end finishes given the neighborhood's price point.

Palma Ceia (33629)

Large lots and established homes on tree-lined streets between Bayshore Boulevard and MacDill Avenue. Palma Ceia homes were built primarily in the 1930s-1960s. Common projects: whole-home renovations that update electrical panels (many older homes still run 100-amp panels — modern kitchens with multiple appliances need 200-amp), open up compartmentalized floor plans, and replace aging plumbing.

Bayshore Beautiful (33611)

Homes along and near Bayshore Boulevard — one of the longest continuous sidewalks in the world at 4.5 miles. Some Bayshore properties are in flood zones. Waterfront and near-waterfront homes often require elevation certificates and flood zone compliance for permitted work. Bayshore renovations frequently include impact-rated windows to meet current Florida Building Code wind-borne debris requirements.

Westshore (33609)

A mixed residential and commercial area near Tampa International Airport. Westshore homes are generally 1950s-1970s construction — CBS (concrete block stucco) single-family homes and townhomes. Common projects include kitchen modernization, bathroom updates, and converting enclosed garages or carports into living space.

Beach Park, Ballast Point & Harbour Island

Beach Park (33609) offers mid-century homes with waterfront access on Hillsborough Bay. Ballast Point (33611) surrounds Ballast Point Park with a mix of original 1950s homes and renovated properties. Harbour Island (33602) is a planned community with condos and townhomes — we handle interior condo renovations including kitchen and bath remodels within HOA guidelines.

Permitting in South Tampa

South Tampa is within the City of Tampa limits. Permits are issued through the City of Tampa Construction Services Division (tampa.gov/guide/permits).

  • Requires a permit: Structural changes, electrical work, plumbing changes, window/door replacement, HVAC modifications, additions
  • No permit needed: Cabinet refacing, countertop replacement, painting, flooring, hardware changes
  • Typical permit cost: $200-$800 depending on scope (per City of Tampa)
  • Approval timeline: 10-15 business days for standard residential permits (City of Tampa)
  • Hyde Park ARC review: Additional 2-4 weeks for exterior modifications

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a kitchen remodel cost in South Tampa? A mid-range kitchen renovation in South Tampa — new cabinets, quartz countertops, flooring, appliances, and lighting — runs $45,000-$80,000. High-end gut renovations with layout changes, custom cabinetry, and premium appliances range from $80,000-$140,000 (per OpenPR and NovaCoreBuilders). South Tampa runs 15-25% above the Tampa metro average because older homes require more electrical, plumbing, and structural work.

Do I need ARC approval for a kitchen remodel in Hyde Park? No — the Hyde Park Architectural Review Commission only reviews exterior changes (windows, doors, additions, roofline modifications, exterior paint). Interior kitchen and bathroom remodels do not require ARC review. However, you still need a City of Tampa building permit if the project involves plumbing, electrical, or structural changes. Permit cost: $200-$800.

How long does a whole-home renovation take in South Tampa? A full whole-home renovation in South Tampa typically takes 12-24 weeks depending on scope. Older homes (pre-1960) often uncover hidden issues during demolition — outdated wiring, galvanized plumbing, termite damage — that add 2-4 weeks. We build a 10-15% contingency into every South Tampa estimate to account for these discoveries.

Are South Tampa homes in flood zones? Some are. Properties along Bayshore Boulevard, on Davis Islands, and in low-lying areas near Hillsborough Bay are in FEMA flood zones AE or VE. Flood zone properties require elevation certificates, flood-resistant materials below the base flood elevation, and flood insurance. Additions to flood zone homes must comply with current FEMA and Florida Building Code elevation requirements. We verify your flood zone status during the initial consultation — it takes 5 minutes to check.

What is the ROI on remodeling in South Tampa? South Tampa has some of the highest home values in Hillsborough County, which supports strong remodeling ROI. A mid-range kitchen remodel returns 60-80% of cost at resale (per national remodeling data adjusted for the Tampa market). Minor kitchen remodels under $25,000 return the highest percentage. Whole-home renovations in Hyde Park and Davis Islands — where buyers expect updated homes — often recoup 70-85% of the investment because the alternative is a lower sale price for an unrenovated property.
